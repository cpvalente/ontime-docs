Ontime Cloud can give anyone a **read-only link** to your show. \
Send it to crew, clients, talent, or a lobby screen: they follow along in a browser, without an Ontime account and without a way back into your running stage.

## Why share a view instead of the stage link?

Your [stage link](/ontime-cloud/manage-stages/) opens the full Ontime app. \
Anyone who has it can reach the editor, and the only thing in the way is the stage password or Pin Code.

A share is a separate, safer link:

- It shows **only** the rundown or timeline, never the editor or the stage itself.
- It has its **own password and expiry**, so you can hand it out widely and revoke it without touching the stage.
- You can create **several links** for the same show, one per audience, and delete each one independently.

See [Sharing flows](/concepts/sharing-flows/) for the bigger picture of access in Ontime.

## Common use cases

| You want to… | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Let crew follow the running order on their phones | Stage rundown share |
| Show a client how the show is tracking, without edit access | Stage rundown share |
| Put the whole event on a lobby or green room screen | Overview share |
| Give a producer one link for every room at a multi-stage event | Overview share |
| Send a client the final rundown for sign-off after the show | Archive share |
| Keep a rundown available after you stop or delete the stage | Archive share |
| Hand talent a fixed running order that won't change under them | Archive share |

## Types of shares

There are three types of share. \
Two are **live** and follow the show as it runs. One is an **archive**, frozen at the moment you captured it.

| | Stage rundown share | Overview share | Archive share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shows** | The rundown table of one stage | A timeline of several stages | The rundown of one archive |
| **Data** | Live | Live | Frozen snapshot |
| **Needs a running stage** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Created from** | Shares page or a stage's menu | Shares page or the Overview page | Project archive page |

### Stage rundown share

A live, read-only view of the rundown of **one stage**.

Viewers can:

- follow the running order as it changes
- choose which columns to show, 12h or 24h time, and whether to show seconds
- refresh on demand, or auto refresh every 1, 5 or 10 minutes
- export the rundown to PDF
- switch between rundowns, if the project has more than one

The badge on the page tells viewers how fresh the data is, for example `Readonly · synced 2 minutes ago`.

### Overview share

A live timeline that puts **several running stages side by side**, with a wall clock.
It is made for screens and for anyone who needs to see the whole event at a glance.

When you create it, you pick which stages appear.
If you pick none, the share shows **all running stages**, including stages you start later.

### Archive share

A read-only view of an [archive](#archive-vs-live-which-one-to-use): a snapshot of a stage's project, taken at the moment you captured it.

The link shows the rundown **exactly as it was captured**. It never follows later changes to the stage, and it keeps working after the stage is stopped or deleted.
Viewers get the same rundown tools as a stage rundown share, and the badge reads `Archive · captured …` so nobody mistakes it for live data.

## Archive vs live: which one to use?

The difference is **when** the viewer sees the data.

- A **live share** (stage rundown or overview) shows the show **now**. It changes as the operator edits the rundown and as the show runs.
- An **archive share** shows the show **as it was** at one moment. It never changes.

**Use a live share when**

- the show is running, or about to run
- viewers need to react to changes: delays, reordered events, new notes
- the stage will stay running for as long as people need the link

**Use an archive share when**

- you want sign-off on a specific version of the rundown
- the show is over and you want to stop the stage, but keep the rundown available
- you are about to make changes and don't want viewers to see work in progress
- you need a record of what was planned, or what actually happened

:::tip[Combine them]
Capture an archive before you start editing a rundown someone has approved. \
Share the archive for reference, and keep the live share for the people who need to see changes as they happen.
:::

## Create a share

Manage all your shares from the **Shares** page in the dashboard.

### Share a stage rundown

<Steps>

1. Start the stage you want to share.

2. Open the stage's menu and choose **Create rundown share**. \
   Alternatively, go to **Shares** and click create under **Stage rundown shares**, then pick the stage.

3. Give the link a name, so you can recognise it later. It defaults to the stage title.

4. Optionally, choose a **Link expiry** and tick **Require password**.

5. Click **Create** and copy the link.

</Steps>

Stages with at least one share show a **Shared** badge on the dashboard.

### Share an overview

<Steps>

1. Start the stages you want to show.

2. Go to **Shares** and click create under **Overview shares**. \
   Alternatively, open the **Overview** page, use **Select stages** to pick the stages you want, then click **Share this view**.

3. Choose which stages to include. Leave all unticked to include every running stage.

4. Name the link and optionally set an expiry and a password.

5. Create the share and copy the link.

</Steps>

### Share an archive

<Steps>

1. With the stage running, open the stage's menu and choose **Create archive**. \
   This captures the project as it is right now.

2. Go to **Project archive** and open the menu of the archive you want to share.

3. Choose **Create share link**.

4. Name the link and optionally set an expiry and a password.

5. Click **Create link** and copy the link.

</Steps>

## Manage your shares

From the **Shares** page you can open, copy, edit or delete every link.

- **Rename** a link, or **change its password** and **expiry**. Choosing `No change` keeps the current expiry.
- For overview shares, **change which stages** are included. The link stays the same.
- **Delete** a link to revoke it straight away. Anyone who opens it afterwards gets a "not found" page.

Each page shows how many links you are using, for example `1 of 2 used`.
The number of shares you can create depends on your plan, see the [Ontime Cloud plans](https://www.getontime.no/pricing).

## What your viewers see

**Password-protected links** ask for the password once. The browser then remembers it for 24 hours.
Remember to send the password along with the link.

**Expired or deleted links** show a "not found" page.

**When a stage is stopped**, viewers of its live shares see a **Share unavailable** page. The same link works again as soon as you start the stage.

**When a stage is deleted**, its stage rundown shares are deleted too, and it is removed from any overview share.
If you need the rundown after deleting the stage, capture and share an archive first.

**When an archive is deleted**, its share links are deleted with it.

:::note[Live, but not instant]
Live shares read from your stage at most about once a minute. They are great for following a running order, but they are not a replacement for the second-by-second views in Ontime, like the [Operator](/interface/production/operator/) or a [stage timer](/interface/automated/stage-timer/).
:::

:::note[Network hiccups]
If the stage is briefly unreachable, a live share keeps showing the last data it had, and the badge changes to `Offline · last synced …`. \
The view recovers on its own once the stage is reachable again. It only closes if something permanent changes, like the share being deleted or expiring.
:::
